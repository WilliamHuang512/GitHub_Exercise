Why this topic matters to me

In only about three years, tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini went from a curiosity to something many students open every day. I've watched this shift happen in my own classes and among my friends. The same tool that can explain a confusing concept in seconds can also write an entire assignment, and most students I know feel both excited and uneasy about that. Universities are still figuring out what responsible use looks like, and students themselves are split on whether AI is helping or hurting how they learn.

I'm interested in this question because it isn't abstract for me. As a student taking a data visualization course, I use these tools, I see their strengths, and I also notice the moments where it would be easy to stop thinking for myself. Rather than relying only on headlines or opinion pieces, I wanted to look at what students actually do and how they actually feel, and whether those feelings have changed over time.

That curiosity became the focus of my DS4200 group project, which I'm working on with Chloe Murray, Emma Kulla, and Peng-Yu Chen. We are designing a survey of college students aged 18 to 23, mostly at Northeastern, to measure how much they use AI, why they use it, and how they think it affects their independent thinking. Our main question is whether the students who rely on AI the most for school are also the ones who feel it is costing them their learning. On the next page you can read more about the project, our survey design, and some of the existing research that motivated it, including an interactive D3 chart.
